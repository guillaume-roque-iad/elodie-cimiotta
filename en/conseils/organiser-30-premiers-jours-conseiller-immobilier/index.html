How should you organise your first 30 days as an independent property adviser?

Published 22 September 2026
Team Lucky editorial team · article prepared with AI assistance

During your first 30 days, do not try to master everything or achieve an immediate commercial result. Build a working foundation instead: clarify the framework, follow your learning path, approach contacts respectfully, organise follow-up and review your actions with your mentor. This plan is neither an income promise nor a timetable for success.

Week 1: understand the framework before speeding up

Start by distinguishing the role you will actually perform. In France, different rules apply to a property agent holding a professional card, an employee, and a self-employed commercial agent or mandatary working under a card holder. Official source: Service Public Entreprendre. For a project in another country, check local rules: this translation does not transpose French law.

List the documents, tools and learning stages relevant to you. Ask who approves your start, where to find current procedures and who handles an urgent question. Then reserve recurring time to learn, practise and review. A useful first week mainly creates clarity.

Week 2: prepare conversations, not a pitch

Onisep associates the role with communication, negotiation, organisation and autonomy, among other skills. Source: Onisep career profile. These are developed in real conversations: listen, ask questions, summarise and agree a next step.

Make a list of people and local contacts you already know without assuming they have a property project. Prepare a simple introduction to your new activity and ask permission to continue the conversation. The aim is not to solicit your entire address book, but to learn to explain your role without pressure or promises.

Week 3: establish a weekly rhythm

A useful diary separates five blocks: learning, contacting, meetings, follow-up and administration. Set the slots before urgent tasks fill the week. Leave room to prepare meetings and record next actions.

iad’s content on professional skills highlights organisation, relationships and confidence with tools. Source: iad France. Turn these ideas into observable actions: update every conversation, schedule a reminder, note a professional question and end the day with a short review.

Week 4: measure activity without confusing action and outcome

Review indicators you can control: completed learning, conversations started, follow-ups scheduled, meetings prepared and questions resolved. They describe consistency; they do not predict revenue or the time until a transaction.

With your mentor, choose one habit to keep, one action to simplify and one skill to develop during the next month. Ask for a concrete example rather than a general phrase: how to prepare a call, follow up tactfully or review a meeting.

Your one-page dashboard

At the end of each week, record what you learned, people contacted with their permission, dated next steps, obstacles and the question to take to the next team discussion. The dashboard should help you decide, not compare yourself with others.

Useful check: if your diary contains only training, add practice. If it contains only outreach, add time to prepare, follow up and learn.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to contact everyone you know in the first week?

No. Start with a selected list and a respectful approach. Explain your activity, ask permission to continue and accept a no without insisting.

How much time should go to learning and business development?

There is no universal ratio. Reserve blocks for both, then adjust to your level, working framework and feedback from your mentor.

What should you prepare before speaking with Élodie?

Bring the country where you plan to work, your real availability, questions about the role and an example of a possible week. A first conversation is offered in French or Spanish, without commitment.
